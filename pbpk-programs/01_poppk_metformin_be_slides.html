Objective & rationale

The question

Can a lightweight PopPK model — the kind you can fit to a sparse clinical BE study — reproduce the bioequivalence conclusions that the full mechanistic PBPK model produces for a modified-release product?

Why it matters

  • PBPK sweeps are expensive and platform-bound (PK-Sim).
  • Regulatory BE decisions are made on NCA of observed concentrations.
  • If a PopPK surrogate agrees, it validates a cheaper, data-driven route to the same safe space.

Critical quality attribute (CQA)

Metformin XR is a modified-release tablet. The formulation attribute under test is the in-vivo release half-time T50, swept from 1 h (fast) to 7 h (slow). The reference product releases at T50 = 3.0 h. A test batch is bioequivalent if the 90% CI of the GMR for Cmax / AUC lies within 0.80–1.25.

Analysis pipeline

From PBPK output to a fitted PopPK model

  • 1 · Dense PBPK profiles. 8 PK-Sim virtual populations (one per T50), 24 subjects each, 0–24 h.
  • 2 · Sparse-sample each subject to a realistic BE sampling schedule and add 15% proportional residual error → NONMEM-style dataset.
  • 3 · Fit a Pumas PopPK model per formulation arm (FOCE).
  • 4 · Re-simulate dense individual predictions (IPRED).
  • 5 · NCA on IPRED → per-subject Cmax, AUCtEnd, AUCinf.
  • 6 · TOST / VBE → GMR, 90% CI, power, safe space.

PopPK structural model

  • One-compartment, first-order absorption with lag time.
  • Parameters: Ka, CL, Vc, tlag.
  • IIV (log-normal) on Ka, CL, Vc.
  • Proportional residual error.

The same BE machinery (be_utils.jl — TOST, VBE, safe-space) is used here and on the PBPK side, so any difference in conclusion is attributable to the model, not the statistics.

Reading the safe space

The range of T50 values for which a batch would pass BE with ≥80% power. A wider range = more formulation latitude.

What it says

Cmax defines a tight window (~1.8–4.1 h); AUC endpoints tolerate the full slow-release arm. Larger n narrows the low end slightly (more power to detect a fast-release failure).

Conclusions — PopPK analysis

  • A simple 1-compartment PopPK model, fit to sparsely-sampled virtual subjects, cleanly recovers the metformin XR BE structure.
  • Cmax is the binding endpoint. The PopPK safe space is roughly T50 ≈ 1.8–4.1 h (Cmax-limited); AUC endpoints are permissive across the slow-release arm.
  • Flip-flop kinetics are visible in the parameter trends and inflate AUCinf extrapolation at high T50 — treat AUCinf with caution as a BE endpoint for slow-release batches.

Limitations

Virtual (PBPK-derived) data with a fixed 15% proportional error; residual-error and BSV assumptions are stylised. OFV/deviance unavailable in this Pumas build (does not affect NCA-based BE). See the companion deck for head-to-head agreement with the PBPK reference.
