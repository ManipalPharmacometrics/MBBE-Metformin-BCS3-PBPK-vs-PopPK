1Objective & rationale

Metformin XR is a modified-release tablet whose in-vivo release half-time (T50) is the critical quality attribute under test. This analysis asks whether a lightweight population-PK model — the kind fit to a sparse clinical BE study — reproduces the bioequivalence conclusions that a full mechanistic PBPK model gives, across formulations ranging from fast (T50 = 1 h) to slow (T50 = 7 h) release. A test batch is bioequivalent to the reference (T50 = 3.0 h) when the 90% CI of the geometric mean ratio (GMR) for Cmax and AUC lies entirely within 0.80–1.25.

2Data & workflow

Eight PK-Sim PBPK virtual populations (one per T50, 24 subjects, 0–24 h) were sparse-sampled to a realistic BE schedule and given 15% proportional residual error, producing a NONMEM-style dataset. A Pumas PopPK model was fit per arm, dense individual predictions (IPRED) were re-simulated, non-compartmental analysis (NCA) produced per-subject Cmax / AUC, and the same TOST / virtual-BE machinery used on the PBPK side computed GMRs, power, and the safe space.

Fair-comparison note. The BE statistics (be_utils.jl) are identical to the PBPK pipeline, so any difference in conclusion is attributable to the model, not the test procedure. See the companion PBPK-vs-PopPK deck for the head-to-head.

Per-subject exposure metrics from the dense IPRED profiles. Cmax falls monotonically with slower release; AUC(0–tEnd) is comparatively stable.

The reliability of AUC(inf) degrades as release slows, because the terminal phase becomes absorption-limited and a larger fraction of AUC is extrapolated:

Watch-out. Terminal-phase regressions are excellent (λz R²adj ≈ 1.00 throughout), but rising extrapolation at high T50 means AUC(inf) should be read with caution there.

8Conclusions & limitations

  • A simple 1-compartment PopPK model, fit to sparsely-sampled virtual subjects, cleanly recovers the metformin XR BE structure.
  • Cmax is the binding endpoint. The PopPK safe space is approximately T50 1.9–4.0 h (Cmax-limited); AUC endpoints are permissive across the slow-release arm.
  • Flip-flop kinetics are visible in the parameter trends and inflate AUC(inf) extrapolation at high T50 — treat AUC(inf) with caution as a BE endpoint for slow-release batches.
Limitations. Virtual (PBPK-derived) data with a fixed 15% proportional error; residual-error and BSV assumptions are stylised. OFV/deviance were unavailable in this Pumas build (does not affect NCA-based BE).
